## 0.33.0 - 2026-10-29

Reads document contract **9.33.0** (theme 4.0.1, icon set 1.0.0, data dictionary 1.8.0) and placement map **1.1.0**. Folds in the entries that were prepared as 0.33.0 to 0.37.0 and never published.

- **A place can be scheduled.** An entry of the placement map may carry `schedule`: documents that stand in the place for a window of time (`from` included, `until` excluded, UTC whole seconds), over its permanent content; a window with `documentId: null` pauses the place (`empty`). `resolvePlacementAt`, `nextPlacementBoundary` and `placementInstantMs` are the pure rules (a broken window is skipped whole, an overlap goes to the later `from`, a broken permanent content stays `unreadable`); every row of `scheduleCases` of the shared table runs in the package tests. The clock is the device's: `HotlayoutPlacement` sets a timer at the next boundary (cut to 2^31 - 1 ms and counted again), decides again on the map it holds without reading the network, and decides again when the app returns to the foreground. `HotlayoutPlacement(clock: ...)` and `PlacementSource(now: ...)` inject the clock; `PlacementSource.recompute()` and `PlacementLoad.nextBoundaryMs` are the building blocks for a host with its own widget. `PlacementShow` is exported (the type of `PlacementEvent.show`).

- **A carousel follows a second change of the value made during its own scroll.** When the host changed the stop number twice in a row (for example 2 to 0, and 80 ms later to 1) while the reader was still animating to the first target, the second change was compared with the stop the animation was passing by, judged "already there" and not followed: the value said 1 and the carousel stopped on 0. The carousel now follows from the goal of its last own scroll until that scroll comes to rest; a move by a person (a finger, the wheel) drops that goal.

- **A node fades in and out when its condition changes.** A node with `visibilityTransition: 'fade'` and a `visibleIf` no longer blinks when the condition changes on a shown screen. A node whose condition stops holding stays in the layout and fades out for 120 ms (`cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1)`) and then leaves it; a node whose condition starts to hold waits 120 ms out of the layout and then enters it transparent and fades in for 160 ms (`cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.2, 1)`). Every node follows its own timeline, with no pairing, so two neighbours with mutually exclusive conditions change the layout once. A change during a transition goes to the new goal from the current opacity, the time scaled by the distance. While a node fades out it catches no press, takes no focus and is out of the accessibility tree; a node that is entering is a control from the moment it enters the layout. The children of a `Column`, `Row`, `Container`, `Stack`, `Grid` and a list all fade; the zones of the screen (the first child of the root when it is an app bar, a node with a `pin` of any value, a navigation bar and its items), a tab of `Tabs` and a node without `visibleIf` follow their condition at once. There is no transition on the first show, after going back, when the language or the width class changes, or under the system "disable animations". A document without the field draws exactly as before. `visibilityFadeChange`, `visibilityFadeAt` and the other rules in `visibility_fade.dart` are the pure rules; every row of `fixtures/visibility-fade/visibility-fade-cases.json` runs in the package tests, the screen row on the real finished screen.

- **A carousel tells the active stop.** The optional `carousel.stopPath` names a number data path. When a person's swipe or the wheel has settled on a stop after snapping, the reader writes the stop number (from 0, counted by stops, not cards) to that path through the write a field makes: the session of the screen stack and `onWrite`, under `writablePaths`. `onAction` is not called and there is no analytics event. Nothing is written during the gesture, for a move the reader made itself (the show, following the value), or when the path already holds that number. The carousel stands on the stop the value names when it is shown, without animation (a non-integer or missing value is the first stop, a number beyond the stops is clamped to the last), and moves to it when the number changes — with animation, or at once under the system "disable animations"; a change while a finger holds the row is not applied, and a write the host refused leaves the carousel where the person put it. A card with the stop in a data list is not published by the builder. A document without the field draws exactly as before. `carouselStopPathOf`, `carouselShownStop`, `carouselStopFollow` and `carouselStopWrite` are the pure rules; every row of the `stop...` groups of `fixtures/carousel/carousel-cases.json` runs in the package tests, the run rows with real fingers.

- **A sheet takes the bottom inset inside its box.** A modal sheet from the bottom reaches the physical bottom of its area, so the home indicator or the gesture bar used to lie over its last node (usually the main button). The reader now adds the platform inset (`MediaQuery.viewPaddingOf(context).bottom` of its own context) inside the sheet, under the content, once: the background and the radius of the screen root extend under the inset, and the last node stands above it. With a shown pinned block (`pin: 'bottom'`) the inset goes under the content of that zone, as on a page, not a second time under the root. The sheet is as tall as its content plus the inset, no taller than the area; taller content scrolls the body above the inset. A window in the middle (`modalPlacement: 'center'`), a sheet shown as a window on a wide layout and pages are unchanged. A host that already accounts for the inset (a `SafeArea` above the reader) gives zero and sees no change. `sheetSafeBottomOf`, `sheetInsetPlaceOf` and `sheetBoxOf` are the pure rules; every row of the shared table `fixtures/sheet-safe-bottom/sheet-safe-bottom-cases.json` runs in the package tests.

- **A write action on a long press is performed.** A `setValue` or `toggleValue` in `longPress` of a card or a list row used to be dropped when the document was read; it now runs after the hold (or Shift+F10 / the context menu key) the way the same action runs on a tap: the haptic request, then the analytics event, then the write into the session and `onWrite` (under `writablePaths`). Writing a value the session already holds writes nothing. The role of the node still comes from its `action` alone, so a write on the hold makes the node neither an option of a group nor a switch.
- **The node field `name` is accepted and ignored.** It is a label for the document's author in the editor; it is not drawn, not part of an accessible name, and not passed to actions, events or reports.

## 0.32.0

The contract version is unchanged (9.28.0). Adds placements (a named place of the app that shows a document chosen in the builder) and two rendering fixes.

- **Placements.** A placement is a named place of the app (`home.banner`) whose document is chosen in the builder and published as the placement map, `placements/published.json`. New: `HotlayoutPlacement(name:, projectBase:, theme:, data:, …)` draws the document of a place and draws nothing, telling the host through `onPlacement` with a `PlacementReport`, when the place is empty, unknown or unreadable, or its document is gone or cannot be read; an entry point the document lacks starts on its first screen and is reported as `entryMissing`. `PlacementSource(projectBase:, name:)` reads the map with `ETag` polling, keeps the last known map (optionally in a `PlacementMapStore`) and falls back to a bundled map; `PlacementLoad` carries the `PlacementDocumentSource` (a `DocumentSource`) and the `entry` of the place, and `PlacementSourceStatus` keeps "the network is silent" apart from "the map cannot be read" and "no map was published". `resolvePlacement` and `readPlacementMapText` are the pure rules. The contract artifact `assets/contract/placements.json` (1.0.0) and its case table `fixtures/placements/placement-cases.json` are added to the repository copy of the contract; every row of the table runs in the package tests. Nothing changes for an app that does not use placements.
- **Placement reading rules.** The map body is read as bytes, up to 262 144 (the byte order mark counts); strict UTF-8, never repaired with U+FFFD (invalid is `corrupt`, not `offline`); one leading byte order mark is skipped; nesting deeper than 32 levels is `corrupt`, judged before parsing; the major version is compared as a string of digits, so a version of any length is another major. Redirects are not followed (a `3xx` is no answer for the map and an unreadable document) and every request has 20 seconds, to the end of the body (`kPlacementFetchTimeout`). The last known map is kept per map address: `PlacementMapStore.read(key)` and `write(key, text)` take the address, and `PlacementResponse.body` is now `Uint8List`. A document the widget keeps on screen is remembered by its address and dropped on 404. New `onPlacementEvent` / `PlacementEvent`: the host hears every change of `show`, `report`, `mapStatus`, `documentId` or `entry`, including the return of a drawn document; `onPlacement` keeps hearing only the reasons. `readPlacementMapBytes` reads a body.
- **Placement fixes.** `HotlayoutPlacement(...)` keeps its source in its state and rebuilds it only when the name, the project or the bundled map change (a parent rebuild no longer drops the document and reads the map again); a refresh asked for while one is running runs after it, so a swapped source is read at once. `SchemaVersion.tryParse` returns `null` for a number too big for an int instead of throwing. `HttpDocumentTransport` takes `followRedirects`.
- **A button with a border keeps its shadow.** A `Button` or `IconButton` whose style names a border and a shadow (a white button with a hard shadow under it) was drawn without the shadow: the Material button clipped its background layer, where the shadow is drawn, to its own outline. The shadow now shows under the button, as it does for a button without a border.
- **A window in the middle is centred between the insets.** A screen with `modalPlacement: 'center'` laid edge to edge used to be centred in the area below the top inset only, so on a phone with a home indicator the window stood half the bottom inset too low (about 17 dp on a 393 x 852 phone with insets 59 / 34). The window now stands above the bottom inset as well, as the contract table `modalAreaCases` says; a host that already handles the inset itself (it gives the reader zero) is unaffected. A sheet from the bottom is unchanged.

## 0.30.0

The contract version is unchanged (9.28.0). One new optional parameter; nothing else changes for a host that does not pass it.

- **`onReport`** on `renderDocument` and `renderScreen`: what the reader ignored, replaced or refused on its own now reaches the host in release builds too, not only the debug log. `HotlayoutReport` is a sealed class with the kinds the other readers of the contract share: `IgnoredInputReport` (`where`, `reason`: an unknown widget or token, a refused picture address, a write path the document had no right to name, a list cut by the budget, a screen deeper than the parse limit, a node of the wrong shape), `IncompatibleDocumentReport`, `NewerMinorDocumentReport`, `UnknownEntryReport`, `InvalidThemeValuesReport` (`paths`), `ThemeFallbackReport` and `UnknownThemeIdReport`. The list budget used to print on every build; it is now one report per screen. At most 50 distinct reports are delivered per widget. No report carries a value from your data.
- **Findings made before the widget exists** (parsing a document, reading a theme) travel with the parsed object: `UiDocument.reports` and `RenderTheme.reports`. `renderDocument` delivers both; `renderScreen` delivers the theme's, so a host that draws single screens reads `UiDocument.reports` itself. `ThemeContract.themeById` for an unknown id and a `ThemeLoad` that fell back return themes that carry the matching report.
- The debug log lines are kept, printed once per distinct message.

## 0.29.0

Reads document contract **9.28.0** (theme 4.0.1, icon set 1.0.0 and data dictionary unchanged). The version adds no field: it states rules of the reader. A document that stays inside the limits is drawn exactly as by 0.28.0.

- **A picture body is read up to 10 485 760 bytes** (10 MiB; `kDefaultMaxDownloadBytes` was 10 000 000). Redirects (at most three, each target judged again by the address policy), the 30 seconds for a picture with its redirects, and the decode of the longer side at no more than 4096 pixels with the proportion kept are as in 0.28.0.
- **The host's decision cannot make a script a picture.** An address that `resolveImageUrl` returns and that a URL parser reads as `javascript:` or `vbscript:` (leading controls and spaces stripped, tab and line breaks removed, any case) is refused like any other refused address.
- **A refused picture is reported** in debug builds: the address of the document up to 120 characters, for an address from your data only its scheme and host.
- **A list budget.** A screen draws at most 2550 instances of list templates, nested lists included, counted in document order, depth first. The count follows the whole tree of the screen for its width class, not what is shown now: a hidden list, a list in an inactive tab or in a folded block counts the same. The instance that would pass the limit is not drawn, nor anything inside it or after it on the screen. A debug line says how many instances were left out. A modal sheet and the page under it have a budget each.
- **Numbers of a theme outside the schema bounds, or not finite, no longer drop the token.** The value of the same token and field in the built-in theme of the same brightness is used for spacing, radius, font size, line height, font weight and letter spacing; every other number (a custom token, a field the built-in token lacks, a shadow layer, a gradient stop) takes the nearest bound. `RenderTheme.numberReports` lists every replacement as `<place>: built-in` or `<place>: bound`. A value that is not a number still leaves the token out.
- **A text that paints nothing says nothing.** A text whose colour (its own or inherited) is fully transparent, or that is inside a node whose resolved opacity is exactly `0`, is out of the semantics tree, so it is not part of the name of a pressable card. A card and a control keep their own names. `0.01` is still heard.
- **A sheet with `dismissible: false`** takes no focus trap and no Esc; the system back stays with the host. Nothing changes for a layer that can be dismissed.

## 0.28.0

Hardening release: the document, the theme and the data arrive from the network, and the package now treats all three as untrusted input. The contract version is unchanged (9.27.0). New parameters are optional; the changed defaults are listed first.

**Changed defaults**

- **A picture is fetched only from an `https` address whose host is a name** (a host with an alphabetic top-level label, the host form of `openUrl`). An IP address in any notation (`127.0.0.1`, `2130706433`, `0x7f000001`, `169.254.169.254`), an IPv6 address, a single-word host such as `localhost`, an address with user info and every other scheme draw the "image unavailable" placeholder and make no request. This covers pictures from the document and from your data, raster and SVG. `data:image/…` addresses are not network requests and still draw (see the size limit below). If your pictures live on an internal host, pass `resolveImageUrl`.
- **A picture body is read up to 10 MB** (`maxDownloadBytes`). A larger `Content-Length` is refused before the body is read, and a body that does not declare its length is cut at the limit.
- **A picture is decoded at the size of its box**, never above 4096 pixels on a side and never enlarged, so a small file that declares a huge size cannot allocate gigabytes of pixels.
- **A picture request gives up after 30 seconds** and sends no cookies, no credentials and no headers. Redirects are followed by hand, at most three, and every target goes through the address policy again.
- **A document or theme body is read up to 4 MiB** (four times the contract's 1 MiB budget) by `HttpDocumentTransport`. A larger answer is a `malformed` load with the bundled copy (`DocumentLoadStatus.malformed`, `ThemeLoadStatus.malformed`).
- **Theme numbers are read within the bounds of the theme schema** (font size 8…96, line height 0.8…3, letter spacing −2…8, font weight 100…900, spacing 0…128, radius 0…256, shadow offset and spread −64…64, shadow blur 0…128, opacity and gradient position 0…1). A token with a number outside its bounds, or one that is not finite, is left out as if it were malformed, and the built-in value applies.
- **`ThemeLoad.fromJson`, `parse` and `fromNetwork` never throw**: a body the reader cannot handle is a `malformed` load with the built-in theme.
- **Nothing from your data is written to the log any more**: the SVG failure line (which used to carry the address, in release builds too) now names only the class of the failure (`HTTP 404`, `timeout`, `parse error`, …) and is printed in debug builds only; the debug notices about an unknown icon name and an unmatched choice no longer print the value.

**New parameters** of `renderDocument`, `renderScreen` and `ScreenFlow`

- `resolveImageUrl: String? Function(String url)`: your decision about every picture address, `data:` addresses included. Return the address to fetch (it may be rewritten) or `null` to refuse it. A policy that throws refuses. It is called again for every redirect target.
- `maxDownloadBytes: int`: the most bytes of one picture body (default 10 000 000).
- `writablePaths`: the data paths the document may write, as a `bool Function(String path)` or an `Iterable<String>`. A write to any other path (by a field or by `setValue` / `toggleValue`) reaches neither the renderer's session nor `onWrite`. Without it every well-formed path is allowed, as before.

**Fixes**

- A number that is infinite (`1e400` in JSON) in `maxLength`, `titleMaxLines`, `subtitleMaxLines`, a field radius or the size of a part no longer throws: an integer field takes a number only when it is finite and whole. The same guard covers the other places where a document number becomes an integer (filled steps of a segmented bar, the fill line of a bar with steps, the dashes of a dashed border).

## 0.27.0

Reads document contract **9.27.0** (theme 4.0.1 and icon set 1.0.0 unchanged; data dictionary 1.7.0). Two minors in one release: 9.26.0 (a long press, a grouped list, a sheet that closes by a drag) and 9.27.0 (the record of a page follows the data). A document without the new fields is drawn exactly as by 0.26.1, except that a modal sheet now follows the finger down (see below).

- **A long press** (`longPress` and `longPressLabel` on a `Column`, `Row`, `Container`, `Stack` and `ListTile`). It has the form of `action` and runs the same way: `navigate` inside the document, everything else to `onAction` / `onRecordAction` with the record of the node, and the haptic request, the analytics event and the action of the long press in that order. It fires 450 ms after the finger went down, while it is still down, and the lifted finger presses nothing. A finger that moves farther than 10 dp first is neither a long press nor a press. A node without a `longPress` takes a long hold as a press, as before. The innermost control takes the hold as it takes the press, so a button in a card keeps its own press and a row with its own long press inside a card gets it. A card or a row with only a long press is still a control (focused, named, sinks under the finger, its tap does nothing); a write action on a long press is not run; a row that is an option of a choice does not run it. The pressed pose stays while the finger is down. Accessibility: a custom action named by `longPressLabel` (the platform's own long press action when the name cannot be heard); the context menu key and Shift+F10 on the focused node perform it at once.
- **A grouped list** (`Repeater.groupBy` with a second child as the header). Records that follow one another with the same value under the path form a group, made of the records the list draws (after `where` and `limit`); the header is drawn before each group in the scope of the group's first record, and a press inside it carries that record. Absent, `null` and `""` are one value, and `1` and `"1"` are two. Headers and records stand in one flow with one `gap`; a header hidden by its condition takes no place; numbered items count records only. A grid, a row, a carousel and a list without a header child are drawn without headers.
- **A sheet that closes by a drag down.** A modal sheet from the bottom (not a window in the middle) follows the finger down when its body is scrolled to the top (otherwise the body scrolls first) and never goes above its place. Released at 0.3 of its height, or thrown down at 700 dp per second, it leaves in 200 ms and only then does the stack go one step back (`back` event); a fling up, or a shorter drag, returns it in 250 ms. The scrim fades with the part of the sheet still seen. The finger that dragged the sheet presses nothing under it. Under `MediaQuery.disableAnimations` the sheet still follows the finger, and the return and the exit are instant (as with `HotlayoutTransitions.none`).
- **`dismissible: false` on the entry of a modal screen** turns off the drag, the tap on the scrim and Esc. The system back stays with the host, and a `navigate.back` inside the layer closes it. `ScreenEntry.dismissible` is the new field.
- **The record of a page follows the data.** The record a frame of the stack holds (opened from a list row, inherited by a sheet over it, carried by `carryRecord`) is now the chain of the rows it came from. Whenever you give new `data`, every frame looks for its rows by `<item>.id`, first in the collection the list read and then in the other collections the document names for the same entity, and takes the first match whole; a row found nowhere keeps the last record it had, and a row with no `id` stays as it was opened. The stack, `onScreenShown` and the analytics do not change; the screen keeps its scroll, carousels and typed text; a changed number counts to its new value (`animateChanges`); the record handed to `onRecordAction` is the refreshed one.
- Shared tables run by this release, every row by the rule and, where a screen can show it, through real widgets: `long-press-cases.json` (`gestureCases` 11, `keyCases` 6, `nodeCases` 9, `receiverCases` 5, `documentCases` 7, `ruleCases` 5), `repeater-group-cases.json` (`sameCases` 11, `groupCases` 7, `listCases` 6, `documentCases` 8, `ruleCases` 3), `sheet-dismiss-cases.json` (`startCases` 8, `releaseCases` 9, `entryCases` 2, `documentCases` 3, `ruleCases` 6) and the groups `entitySourceCases` (3), `liveRecordCases` (23) and `liveFlowCases` (6) of `record-scope-cases.json`. Fixtures `longpress-groups-sheet-screens` and `live-record-screens` are in the fixture catalog.
- For widget tests: a hold of 450 ms needs `pump(Duration)` (the timer is a real `Timer`); a drag inside a sheet loses the first 18 dp to the touch slop, as under any button, because the sheet's own drag stands in the gesture arena next to the body's scroll view. `ScreenFlowController.frameRecords` (`@visibleForTesting`) lists the record of every frame of the stack.

## 0.26.0

Reads document contract **9.25.0** (theme 4.0.1, icon set 1.0.0 and data dictionary 1.5.0 unchanged). Five optional additions; a document without them is drawn exactly as by 0.25.0, except that pressable nodes now sink under the finger (see below).

- **Steps that fill smoothly.** While the value of a `ProgressBar` with `segments` runs a transition (`animateChanges`, `animateIn`) the filled steps are a number with a fraction, `U(from) + (U(to) - U(from)) * e`, where `U` is the number of whole steps at rest and `e` the curve of the transition. The step that is filling is drawn as its track (or `currentColor`) with the fill over it, cut at its share, with the radius of the step kept; the gradient runs along the drawn fill. At rest the steps are whole, both ends of a transition are pictures at rest, a change inside one step moves nothing, and `MediaQuery.disableAnimations` gives whole steps at once.
- **The glow of a scale** (`glow: 'soft' | 'strong'` on `ProgressBar` and `CircularProgress`): a blurred copy of the paint of the fill (its colour, or its gradient along the fill, with the round ends) lies under the scale. `soft` is a Gaussian blur of 4 dp at opacity 0.55, `strong` of 8 dp at 0.9. The track does not glow, nothing filled means no glow, and the glow takes no room and is not clipped by the box of the node. In a bar with steps one step glows: the current one in `currentColor` when that colour is there, otherwise the last step with any fill. It follows the fill during a transition.
- **A shadow of the colour of the background** (`style.shadowColor: 'background'`): every layer of `style.shadow` takes the hue of the node's own background and keeps its own opacity. A gradient gives the colour of the stop nearest to the middle (the earlier one on a tie); no background, an image, or a colour the theme does not know leaves the shadow in its own colour. On a `Button` and a `Badge` it goes with `shadow` to the surface of the role.
- **Press feedback.** The node that performs the action of a press sinks under the finger: a card, a row and an option to 0.97, a `Button` to 0.96, and an `IconButton`, a `ghost` button and a `NavItem` fade to 0.7. Down in 80 ms, back in 160 ms with a small overshoot; a press shorter than the way down still reaches the pose. Painting only, no layout change. Only the innermost control answers (a card around a button stays still); no feedback for a disabled button, a "swipe to confirm" button, the keyboard, or a node with `pressFeedback: 'none'`; `MediaQuery.disableAnimations` keeps only the fade to 0.7.
- **A haptic request** (`haptic` on an action: `light`, `medium`, `heavy`, `selection`, `success`, `warning`). The new callback `onHaptic(HapticWord word, String nodeId, Map<String, Object?>? record)` of `renderDocument`, `renderScreen` and `ScreenFlow` is called once per press, before the analytics event and the action of the same press, also for `none` and for a navigation that finds no target. Nothing for a disabled control, a held key, a drag that has not completed, an unknown word or no field. The reader never vibrates by itself, and the action handed to `onAction` does not carry the field. `HapticWord` is exported.
- Shared tables run by this release, every row by the rule and, where a screen can show it, through real widgets: `segmentFillCases` (12), `segmentFillInlineCases` (4), `transitionShareCases` (5), `glowStepCases` (7), `glowRuleCases` (6), `middleStopCases` (5), `tintCases` (7), shadow-colour `ruleCases` (2), press-feedback `kindCases` (10), `poseCases` (3), `ruleCases` (5), `hapticCases` (7) and `hapticSchemaCases` (4). Fixture `glow-press-screens` is in the fixture catalog.
- For widget tests: a tapped card or button is scaled until a moment after the finger is lifted, so advance time with `pump(Duration)` or `pumpAndSettle` before measuring it. The press feedback is made of a `Transform` inside a `FadeTransition`; a node's own `style.opacity` is still the only `Opacity`.

## 0.25.0

Reads document contract **9.24.0** (theme 4.0.1, icon set 1.0.0 and data dictionary 1.5.0 unchanged). Four optional additions; a document without them is drawn exactly as by 0.24.0.

- **A value that grows when shown** (`animateIn: true` on `Text`, `ProgressBar`, `CircularProgress`). Each time the node is drawn on a shown screen (the first screen, a page pushed or shown again by "back", a section switch, a node that appears by a condition or as a new record) its number grows from zero over the curve, duration and steps of `animateChanges`, together with the slide of the page. A screen under a modal layer is not shown again when the layer closes, and a change of data is not a show. A scale grows its `value` (a literal one too) and its `max` stands at once. With `animateChanges` a change of data during the appearance leads from the shown value; without it the new value stands at once. `MediaQuery.disableAnimations`, a value that is not a number and a value of zero give the value at once. The screen reader hears the final value.
- **"Swipe to confirm" stays busy after the action.** The action runs at once as before; the thumb glides to the end (200 ms) and stays there with a turning ring of three quarters (800 ms a turn), and a press, a gesture and a key do nothing. The callback you pass as `onAction` / `onRecordAction` may return a `Future` (`HostActions.handle` returns the `Future` of its handler): the thumb returns over 250 ms when it completes or fails. Without a `Future` it returns after eight seconds, unless the screen changed first. `none` and an invalid standard action return the thumb at once. A disabled button is as before.
- **The motion of the dot on a navigation item.** When the condition of `badgeWhen` changes its answer while the screen is shown the dot appears with a flash (0 to 1.25 to 1 over 320 ms) or fades (200 ms, in place until the end). No motion on the first show or when the screen is shown again, and none with `MediaQuery.disableAnimations`.
- **The top zone** (`pin: 'top'`). ## 0.4.0

Reads document contract **9.4.0** (theme contract unchanged, 4.0.1; icon set unchanged, 1.0.0) and data dictionary 1.2.0.

- **Sentences with places** (schema 9.4.0). A text can be `kind: 'template'`: a translatable string with named places (`{done} / {total} steps`) and a map of values. - **Changed look:** the ten original icons (`star`, `trophy`, `gift`, `target`, `clock`, `check`, `chevron`, `user`, `info`, `lock`) are no longer Material Icons. They are the drawings of the builder's canvas: a filled star, cup, lock and silhouette, a bolder check and chevron. The chevron of a list row, the chevron of an `Accordion` (it now points to the end of the line and turns down when open, instead of a "down" glyph that flipped up) and the back arrow of `AppBar` are the contract's `chevron` too. Screens you already published look different in this version; that is the purpose of the change.
- Icons are drawn as vector paths by the package's own reader of the icon format (seven SVG elements, every path command), not by `flutter_svg` and not from a font. The icon is on screen in the first frame, takes the foreground colour of its node (`style.color`, own or inherited) for both stroke and fill, and a drawing is parsed on first use and kept. The stroke grows with the icon. The box of an icon and the size of its glyph did not change.
- **Right-to-left:** the 29 icons of `x-mirroredIcons` (arrows, chevrons, `log-in`, `undo`, `send`, `list`, …) are mirrored, once, by the renderer. Everything else is not (`play`, `refresh-cw`, `list-ordered`, `truck`).
- **Breaking (behaviour):** a name the renderer does not have no longer becomes `star`. It leaves an empty box of the same size (`unknownIcon: 'empty-box-same-size'`), in `Icon` and in both slots of `ListTile`. The same for a value that is not a string — an object, a number, `null` (`unreadableIconValue: 'as-unknown-icon'`); the document is still read whole. A missing `Icon.props.name` is still the default of the field, and a missing `trailing.icon` is still "no icon, no box".
- New: `kIconSetVersion` (`1.0.0`) — the version of the drawings built into the package. It is independent of the document contract version, and a document does not carry it.
- New file in the package: `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md`. The drawings, except the ten original ones, are from Lucide (ISC License; some derive from Feather, MIT License). Lucide is not a dependency of the package.
- `kSupportedSchemaVersion` is `9.3.0`.

## 0.2.0

Reads document contract **9.2.0** (theme contract unchanged, 4.0.1) and data dictionary 1.1.2.

- **A list row now carries its record** (schema 9.2.0, "which record does the screen opened from a row show"). An action carries the record of its node; `navigate` from a list row opens the screen *with that row's record*; a page opened by a button outside a list opens without one, a modal sheet keeps the record of the screen beneath; going back drops the record with the screen. Data layers of a screen, strongest first: **record → the user's choice in a bound input → `scopeFor` → `data`**; `null` and a missing key mean "none" and the next layer answers.
- New: `renderDocument(onRecordAction: …)`, `ScreenFlow(onRecordAction: …)`, `renderScreen(onRecordAction: …, record: …)` and the typedef `SduiRecordActionCallback = void Function(ButtonAction action, String nodeId, Map<String, Object?>? record)`. The record is a flat, unmodifiable map of data paths (`{'item.id': 'bottle', 'item.name': 'Bottle'}`), the same shape as `source.record` in `@hotlayout/react`; only keys that have the form of a data path are kept. When both `onAction` and `onRecordAction` are given, only `onRecordAction` is called. **`onAction` keeps its two-argument signature**, so existing hosts compile and behave as before (a new optional input instead of a changed typedef, because a two-parameter function is not assignable to a type with a third parameter).
- **Breaking (rare):** `ScreenScopeRequest` gained a field, `record` — the record the screen was opened with (`null` when it was opened without one). Hosts that only read `request.params` / `request.read` are not affected; hosts that build or destructure the record by pattern must add the field.
- **Breaking:** the path `params.*` is gone. It was a private extension of this renderer that the contract never had (and that the builder does not let a document use). `navigate.params` are a free map of strings *for the host* (`ScreenScopeRequest.params`, `ButtonAction.params`); they are not data of the screen. A row of a list now tells the host *which record* it is through `record`, not through parameters, which are the same in every instance of a row template.
- **Breaking:** the record of a list row is stronger than what the user chose in a bound input (it used to be weaker, so `item.selectedId` was read from the session for every row). A `ChoiceGroup` bound to a path the record also carries still shows the user's pick for as long as the value under its binding is unchanged.
- Language choice, refinements of contract 9.1.0: the locale for dates is the longest well-formed prefix of the requested tag (`ja_JP_JP` → `ja-JP`); `LocaleResolution.directionLocale` is a canonical tag (`ks_IN` → `ks-IN`); a tag longer than 100 characters is not a tag; a language listed in `localization.locales` without a single translated string is not a language of the document (`DocumentLocalization.availableLocales`).
- Data dictionary 1.1.2: the sample images of `item.image` are `data:image/webp`.
- `kSupportedSchemaVersion` is `9.2.0`.

## 0.1.0

First version prepared for integrators. Reads document contract 9.1.0 and theme contract 4.0.1.

- One public library, `package:hotlayout_flutter/hotlayout_flutter.dart`. Added to it: `DocumentLocalization`, `LocalizedStrings`, `LocaleResolution`, `directionOfLocale`, `isRtlLocale`, `VersionVerdict`, `kSupportedSchemaVersion`, `kSupportedThemeContractVersion`, `kDocumentFetchTimeout`, `kThemeFetchTimeout`, `ScreenPresentationToken`, `UiNode`.
- `RendererLabels`: the renderer's own words (back arrow, failed image, version notices) are English by default and can be replaced through the new `labels` parameter of `renderDocument` and `renderScreen`.
- `DocumentLoad.detail`, `ThemeLoad.detail`, the default title of `PublishedDocumentSource` and debug logs are English; logs are prefixed with `[hotlayout]`.
- `CollectionCatalog` and `CollectionSource` are no longer exported: they are host code of the demo app, not part of the renderer.
- `example/`: the quickstart as a complete app.
- Device locale matching of contract 9.1.0: `ar-EG` finds a document's `ar`; never the other way round.
- The package declares no assets. The demo app moved out of the package root into its own app (`demo/` in the repository, not published), so contract fixtures no longer travel into the bundle of every host app.
- **Breaking:** `ThemeLoad.fromBundle` requires `assetPath` — the default pointed at an asset of the demo app and always gave `assetMissing` in a host. `ThemeLoad.fromNetwork` takes an optional `assetPath`; without it the fallback is the built-in theme.
- **Breaking:** `BundledDocumentSource.fixture` is removed (it named assets of the demo app).
- The system back button is connected to the document's screen stack. `ScreenFlow` (and `renderDocument`) handle it through `PopScope`: one screen back inside the document while there is one, your route on the first screen; predictive back on Android 14+ gets the right animation. New: `ScreenFlowController` (`canGoBack`, `currentScreenId`, `goBack()`, a `Listenable`), `ScreenFlowBackCallback` and the parameters `controller`, `onSystemBack`, `handleSystemBack` of `renderDocument` and `ScreenFlow`.
- **Breaking:** `VersionVerdict.majorMismatch` is split into `newerMajor` and `olderMajor`; `isMajorMismatch` answers the old question. The version notice now has two texts: `RendererLabels.updateRequired` ("Update the app to see this screen.") only for a document of a newer major, and the new `RendererLabels.screenUnavailable` ("This screen is not available right now.") for a document of an older major or with an unreadable version — updating the app does not help there. `RendererLabels.unreadableVersion` is removed (renamed to `screenUnavailable`). The reason and both versions go to the debug log and to `DocumentLoad.detail`.
